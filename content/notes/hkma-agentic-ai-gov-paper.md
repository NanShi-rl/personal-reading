## Overview

The HKMA considers that governance arrangements for agentic A.I. should be risk-based and proportionate. The intensity of governance should reflect, among other things:

- the materiality of the use case;
- the degree of autonomy involved;
- the business objective and authority delegated to the agent;
- the nature of the data, tools and systems that the agent can access;
- the complexity of the agentic workflow and its dependencies;
- the potential impact on customers, the institution and the wider financial system; and
- the reversibility of the actions that the agent may take.


## Key Notes & Highlights

3 types of Agentic AI assets in terms of its owner:

- **Own Agentic AI System** - "an AI may deploy, use or rely upon an internally developed, vendor-provided or group-provided agentic system which has access to its data, systems or processes in connection with its Hong Kong operations, **regardless of whether the system is hosted on its infrastructure**."
- **External Customer A2A Instruction** - "it may accept instructions from an external agent acting under a customer's or counterparty's mandate, such as a corporate customer's agent submitting instructions through the AI's channels."
- **External Third Situation A2A Interaction** - "it may encounter agents acting independently for other market participants at arm's length." (outside the scope of this paper)

3 familiar categories fall outside the agentic AI definition described:
- **conventional automation** - including robotic process automation, whose steps are fully prescribed (rule-based)
- **models performing a single fixed task** within a prescribed workflow, such as extracting data from documents (OCR)
- **retrieval chatbots** or **drafting assistants** that produce content for a person to act on (GenAI)
