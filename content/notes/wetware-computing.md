## What is Wetware?

Comparing to hardware and software, wetware refers to **biological tissues** and systems engineered to participate in computational processes. Unlike conventional computers, these utilize living neurons to process information through complex electrochemical signaling.

Despite the excitement surrounding wetware research, the field faces substantial technical challenges. Unlike silicon circuits, which behave in highly predictable ways, the inherently variable nature of living cells can cause them to change their behavior over time. Neural cultures may develop differently from one experiment to another, making **reproducibility** a significant concern.

One of the main factors behind exploring wetware comes through the efficiency of biological systems in performing extremely complex tasks while consuming very little energy.


## History

From Emory University’s [**“leechulator”**](https://www.emory.edu/EMORY_REPORT/erarchive/1999/September/erseptember.27/9_27_99leech.html) that added up sums through leech neurons back in 1999, to Cortical Labs Inc. releasing their [**CL1**](https://corticallabs.com/cl1) as the first commercially available, programmable biological computer, a lot of the present research around wetware typically combines biological components with electronic interfaces, allowing researchers to stimulate biological networks, measure their responses, and connect them to software systems. In effect, the biological tissue becomes integral as an experimental computational platform.
