## Overview & Context

*The Pragmatic Programmer* is one of the definitive works that reshaped how professional developers view their career and day-to-day discipline. The 20th Anniversary Edition refreshes examples while maintaining core philosophies.

## Core Pillars

### 1. A Pragmatic Philosophy
- **Catastrophic entropy (Broken Windows)**: Leaving small design flaws or unhandled edge cases unchecked signals tolerance for decay.
- **Good Enough Software**: Perfectionism is an enemy of pragmatic delivery. Involve users early and iterate.
- **Knowledge Portfolio**: Learn at least one new language every year, read technical and non-technical books, and diversify knowledge.

### 2. Pragmatic Approach & Architecture
- **ETC (Easier to Change)**: The ultimate litmus test for whether a design or architecture is good.
- **Orthogonality**: Eliminate effects between unrelated things. Decouple components to minimize ripple effects of changes.
- **DRY (Don't Repeat Yourself)**: Every piece of knowledge must have a single, unambiguous, authoritative representation within a system.

## Reflections & Action Items
- Regularly audit personal projects for "broken windows" and technical debt.
- Adopt tracer bullets (end-to-end thin vertical slices) when tackling ambiguous architectural challenges.
